Getting There & Around

Castell de Tamarit sits on the coast between La Mora and Altafulla, about an hour south of Barcelona.

By air

Which airport?

Barcelona El Prat (BCN) is the sensible choice — around 90 km, roughly 1 hour 5 minutes by road on the AP-7. Plenty of flights from the UK and Ireland.

Reus (REU) is only about 20 minutes from the venue, but the flight choice is much thinner and often seasonal. Worth a look if something convenient lines up.

By train

Train to Altafulla-Tamarit

There's a small Renfe station right in the village — Altafulla-Tamarit — on the coastal line between Barcelona and Tarragona. Torredembarra station is the next one along.

Trains from Barcelona Sants are straightforward and cheap, and the station is a short taxi ride from the castle and the beach hotels. If you're travelling light and don't fancy a transfer, this is a genuinely good option.

Transfers

Private transfers from the airport

Fixed-price private transfers from Barcelona Airport start from around €100 per vehicle, with meet & greet and free cancellation up to 24 hours before. A standard car covers up to 4 people; minivans cover 5–8.

Operators worth comparing: Titan Transfers, Zone Transfers, Shuttle Direct, MyTransfers and Zip Transfers. Prices vary with time of year and how early you book.

If a few of you are arriving on similar flights, share a minivan or look into a group coach transfer — it works out far cheaper per person than everyone booking separately.

Local taxis

Getting around once you're here

Altafulla and La Mora are small, and there's no Uber or Bolt. Local radio-taxi firms cover Altafulla, Torredembarra and the surrounding villages.

Book your taxis in advance, particularly for the wedding night — a small fleet serving a wedding plus peak summer season means you don't want to be phoning at 1am hoping. We'll share the numbers we're using closer to the date.

Eating out

Restaurants in Altafulla old town

Wander the lanes of the Vila Closa and you'll find: Ca l'Astut, Cà l'Hermité, La Volta, El Sitio, Via Augusta, La Torreta, La Lleona Salada and Granja Sant Francesc.

If you're staying in the old town, these are all on your doorstep — lovely for a slow lunch.

Eating out

Beachfront & Les Botigues de Mar

Down by the sea, in the old fishermen's quarter: Voramar Cal Vitali, Beach House, La Taberna del Mar, La Mar de Tapes, Racó de la Mora, Mera Mar and La Ona.

Best for sea views, a long lunch and something cold.

Eating out

Other spots nearby

La Tortosina, Restaurant Park, Llebeig, Polini, El Tiberi, Club Marítim de Altafulla, Bar Leo, El Molí (pizza), Suko (sushi) and Tropical Grill.

July is peak season on this coast — if there's somewhere you've got your heart set on, book well ahead.

Things to do

Worth a wander

The walled old town of Altafulla (Vila Closa) with its castle, the fishermen's quarter of Les Botigues de Mar along the beach, and the Roman villa of Els Munts — a UNESCO World Heritage site.

The Gaià river estuary at the edge of town is a small nature reserve and a nice flat walk. Tarragona, with its Roman ruins and old town, is about 15 minutes away and well worth a day.

Beaches

Sand and sea

Altafulla beach and the coves around La Mora are the closest stretches. Beyond the castle at Tamarit there's one of the last remaining coastal pine forests in Catalonia, part of it protected — good for shade and a walk.

All of it is walkable from La Mora and Altafulla, and the beach at Tamarit is right below the venue.
